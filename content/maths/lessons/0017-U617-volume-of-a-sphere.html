Volume of a sphere

By the end you can use the sphere formula the exam gives you, leaving π in the answer.

A sphere is a ball shape. Its volume (the space inside, counted in cubes) depends only on the radius r. The exam gives you this formula: V = 4/3 πr³. You still need to know how to use it.

Radius 3 cm. r³ = 27. 4/3 × 27 = 36. Volume = 36π cm³.

The ball and its tin

Drag the ball to turn it. The tin round it has the same radius and is as tall as the ball is wide. The ball fills two thirds of the tin. That is where 4/3 comes from: two thirds of 2r is 4r/3.

The blue line is the radius. The dashed half completes the diameter, which is twice as long. Questions often give the diameter and expect you to halve it.

Worked example

A sphere has radius 3 cm. Find its volume in terms of π.

Step 1, the radius: r = 3.
Step 2, cube it: 3 × 3 × 3 = 27.
Step 3, the 4/3: 27 ÷ 3 = 9, then 9 × 4 = 36.
Step 4, times π: 36π.
Step 5, the unit: 36π cm³.

The method

  1. Find the radius. Halve the diameter if that is what is given.
  2. Cube the radius: r × r × r.
  3. Divide by 3, then multiply by 4. That is the 4/3.
  4. Multiply by π. Leave π in the answer unless told to use a number.
  5. Add the unit cubed, like cm³.

Paper 1: keep π as a symbol, or use 3 if told. Calculator: 4 ÷ 3 × π × r³, then round as asked. 36π = 113.1 cm³ to 1 decimal place. A hemisphere (half a sphere) is the full volume halved.

Why cube the radius here, when the circle only squared it?

Area counts flat squares, so two lengths are multiplied. Volume counts cubes, so three are: r × r × r.

Watch out

Cube the radius, do not square it. 3³ is 27, not 9.

Halve a diameter first. Diameter 6 cm means r = 3.

Keep the 4/3. πr³ on its own is not the volume.

2. Find the volume of this sphere in terms of pi. The first steps are done. Finish it.

27 ÷ 3, then × 4.

Step 1, r = 6 ÷ 2 = 3. Step 2, 3 × 3 × 3 = 27. Step 3, 27 ÷ 3 = 9, then 9 × 4 = 36. Step 4, 36 × π = 36π. Step 5, 36π cm³.

3. A sphere has diameter 6 cm. Taking π as 3, find its volume in cm³.

Halve the diameter, then cube.

Step 1: r = 6 ÷ 2 = 3. Step 2: 27. Step 3: 27 ÷ 3 = 9, then 9 × 4 = 36. Step 4: 36 × 3 = 108. Step 5: 108 cm³.

4. A cone has radius 3 cm and perpendicular height 4 cm. Find its volume in terms of pi.

A cone is not a sphere. Square the radius, times the height, divide by 3.

Not a sphere: this is a cone, U116. r² = 9. 9 × 4 = 36. 36 ÷ 3 = 12. V = 12 × π = 12π cm³.

5. A globe has diameter 12 cm. Taking π as 3, find its volume in cm³.

Halve the diameter, then cube.

Step 1: r = 12 ÷ 2 = 6. Step 2: 216. Step 3: 216 ÷ 3 = 72, then 72 × 4 = 288. Step 4: 288 × 3 = 864. Step 5: 864 cm³.
