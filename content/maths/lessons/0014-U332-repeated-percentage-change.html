Repeated percentage change

By the end you can apply two percentage changes in a row and tell simple from compound interest.

£200 goes up by 10%, then 10% again. The second 10% comes from the new amount, so it is bigger.

Year 1: 10% of £200 is £20, total £220.

2020202020202020202020

Year 2: 10% of £220 is £22, total £242.

2222222222222222222222

10% then 10% gives £242. One 20% rise gives £240.

Worked example

A car worth £8000 loses 10% of its value each year. Find its value after 2 years.
Step 1, year 1: 10% of 8000 = 800. 8000 − 800 = 7200.
Step 2, year 2, on the new amount: 10% of 7200 = 720. 7200 − 720 = 6480.
Step 3, one line per year, so the answer is the last line: £6480. A single 20% drop would give £6400, which is wrong.

£400 is saved at 5% per year for 2 years. Find the total with simple interest, then with compound interest.
Step 4, simple: 5% of 400 = 20 each year. 2 × 20 = 40. Total £440.
Step 1, compound, year 1: 400 + 20 = 420.
Step 2, year 2: 5% of 420 = 21. 420 + 21 = £441.

The method

  1. Do the first change: find the percentage, add it or take it away. Write the new amount.
  2. Do the second change on the new amount, never on the original.
  3. One line per year or per change. Keep the running total on each line.
  4. Simple interest is different: find the interest once, then multiply by the number of years.

Simple interest is paid on the original only, the same every year. Compound is added to the total and grows.

Why does a 10% rise then a 10% fall not get you back to the start?

The fall is 10% of the bigger number. £200 up 10% is £220. 10% of 220 is 22, so it falls to £198.

Another way to see it: the multiplier

With a calculator, multiply by the multiplier each time. Up 10% is × 1.1: £200 × 1.1 × 1.1 = £242. Three years is 1.1³.

The chain stops matching for simple interest. The interest is the same every year, so find it once and multiply by the years.

Watch out

Adding the percentages. 10% then 10% is not 20%.

Taking the second change from the original. The second step is 10% of 220, not 200.

Try it

Answer from memory, no calculator. Give the number only, in pounds where it is money. The working appears only after you check.

1. £500 goes up by 10%, then by 10% again. Find the final amount. The first steps are done.

Step 1: 10% of 500 = 50, so 500 + 50 = 550. Step 2: 10% of 550 = 55. Step 3: 550 + 55 = …

Add 55 to 550.

Step 1: 10% of 500 = 50, so 500 + 50 = 550. Step 2: 10% of 550 = 55. Step 3: 550 + 55 = 605. £605.

2. £300 is put in a savings account paying 10% compound interest per year. How much is in the account after 2 years? The first step is done.

Step 1: 10% of 300 = 30, so 300 + 30 = 330. Step 2: 10% of 330 = …

10% of 330 is 33. Then add it to 330.

Step 1: 10% of 300 = 30, so 300 + 30 = 330. Step 2: 10% of 330 = 33. Step 3: 330 + 33 = 363. £363.

3. A laptop costs £600 new. It loses 20% of its value every year. What is it worth after 2 years?

20% of 600 first. Write the new value before you start year 2.

Year 1: 20% of 600 = 120, so 600 − 120 = 480. Year 2: 20% of 480 = 96, so 480 − 96 = 384. £384.

4. A coat costs £120. In a sale the price is cut by 30%. Find the sale price.

One change only. 10% of 120 first.

This is U554, a single change. 10% of 120 = 12, so 30% = 36. 120 − 36 = 84. £84.

5. A town has 5000 people. The population rises by 20% in one year, then falls by 20% the next year. What is the population at the end?

Do the rise first and write the new number. The fall is 20% of that.

Up: 20% of 5000 = 1000, so 5000 + 1000 = 6000. Down: 20% of 6000 = 1200, so 6000 − 1200 = 4800. It does not go back to 5000.

Teach it back

A £3000 motorbike loses 10% of its value each year. Explain to a friend how to find its value after two years. Say why it is not 20% off. Say it to Claude.
