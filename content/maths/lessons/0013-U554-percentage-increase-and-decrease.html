Percentage increase and decrease

By the end you can increase or decrease an amount by a percentage, and check with a multiplier.

Increase means find the percentage, then add it on. A £40 bike goes up by 20%. Each block is 10%, £4.

444444444444

Ten blocks make the original £40, 100%. The two extra blocks are the 20%, £8. New price 40 + 8 = £48. A decrease takes blocks away.

Worked example

A phone costs £240. The price goes up by 15%. Find the new price.
Step 1, find 15% of 240: 10% of 240 = 24, 5% = 12, so 15% = 24 + 12 = 36.
Step 2, increase, so add it on: 240 + 36 = 276.
Step 3, write the unit and check it went up: £276.

A £60 jacket is 30% off in a sale. Find the sale price.
Step 1, find 30% of 60: 10% of 60 = 6, so 30% = 18.
Step 2, decrease, so take it away: 60 − 18 = 42.
Step 3, £42, smaller than £60, so it is a decrease.

The method

  1. Find the percentage of the amount (U349): 10% is ÷ 10, 5% is half of that, 1% is ÷ 100.
  2. Increase: add it to the original amount. Decrease: take it away.
  3. Write the unit. Check: an increase ends bigger than you started, a decrease smaller.
Why find the percentage first instead of adding 15 straight on?

15% means 15 for every 100, not 15 in total. £240 is 2.4 hundreds, so it gets 2.4 lots of 15: 36. Adding 15 is only right for £100.

Another way to see it: the multiplier

With a calculator (Papers 2 and 3), do it in one go. Up 15% makes the new price 115% of the old, and 115% is 1.15: 240 × 1.15 = 276. Down 30% leaves 70%: 60 × 0.7 = 42.

The multiplier stops matching the blocks when the question wants the increase itself. 240 × 1.15 is the new price, so take 240 off for the £36.

Watch out

Stopping at the percentage. 15% of £240 is £36, but the question wanted the new price, £276.

Adding the percentage as pounds. £240 + 15 is wrong. Find 15% of £240 first.
