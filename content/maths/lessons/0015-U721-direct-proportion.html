Direct proportion

By the end you can scale a price or recipe up or down, and pick the better buy.

Direct proportion (when one amount doubles, the other doubles too) links two things that grow together. 4 pens cost £6. Pens on top, cost below.

Divide by 4 for one pen: £1.50. Multiply by 10 for ten pens: £15.

Worked example

5 pens cost £4. How much do 12 pens cost?

Step 1, one pen: 4 ÷ 5 = £0.80.
Step 2, twelve pens: 12 × 0.80 = £9.60.
Step 3, check: more pens, more money. 12 is more than 5, and £9.60 is more than £4. Answer £9.60.

Pasta: 400 g for £1.20 or 1 kg for £2.80. Which is the better buy?

Step 1, the cost of 100 g for each: 120p ÷ 4 = 30p. Then 280p ÷ 10 = 28p.
Step 3, compare: 28p is less than 30p, so the 1 kg bag is the better buy.

The method

The unitary method (find the value of one first):

  1. Divide to find the value of one: cost ÷ number of items.
  2. Multiply by the number you want.
  3. Check the units and that the answer makes sense: more items, more money.

Recipes: the amount for one person, then multiply. Best buy: the cost of the same amount for each offer, lower wins.

Why find the cost of one pen first, when nobody is buying one pen?

One pen is the building block. Know it, and any number of pens is one multiply. Adding pens on as pounds fails: each pen adds 80p, not £1.

Another way to see it

Scaling, as in U753: 4 pens cost £6, so 8 pens cost £12, both × 2.

It stops matching when the multiplier is awkward: 5 to 12 pens is × 2.4, so find one pen.

Watch out

Adding instead of scaling: 12 pens at 5 for £4 is not £11. Find one pen first.

Cost ÷ pens gives one pen: 4 ÷ 5, not 5 ÷ 4.

The cheaper price tag is not always the better buy. Compare the same amount.

Try it

Answer from memory, no calculator. Give the number only, in pounds for money. The working appears only after you check.

Teach it back

A friend missed today. Explain how to find the cost of 9 tickets when 4 cost £26. Then how to pick the better buy. Say it out loud, or write it down.
