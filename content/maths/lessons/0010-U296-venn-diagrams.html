Venn diagrams

By the end you can fill in a two-set Venn diagram and read any region as a probability.

A Venn diagram (two overlapping circles in a box) sorts a group into sets. 20 students were asked about sport: 12 play football, 9 tennis, 5 both.

The overlap holds the 5 who play both. Football only is 12 − 5 = 7, tennis only 9 − 5 = 4. The 4 outside play neither. The box ξ is everyone asked.

Worked example

The 20 students above. One is picked at random. Find the probability they play tennis only.
Step 1, both: 5 goes in the overlap.
Step 2, football only 12 − 5 = 7. Tennis only 9 − 5 = 4.
Step 3, inside the circles 7 + 5 + 4 = 16. Outside 20 − 16 = 4.
Step 4, "tennis only" is the right-hand crescent: 4.
Step 5, probability 4/20 = 1/5.

ξ = {1, 2, 3, ..., 12}. A = even numbers. B = multiples of 3. Place the numbers.
Step 1, both even and a multiple of 3: 6, 12 go in the overlap.
Step 2, A only: 2, 4, 8, 10. B only: 3, 9.
Step 3, outside: 1, 5, 7, 11. Check 4 + 2 + 2 + 4 = 12, all placed.

The method

  1. Write the "both" number in the overlap first.
  2. Take it away from each set's total to get the "only" parts.
  3. Add the three numbers inside the circles. Take that from the total for the outside.
  4. Read the question: "and" is the overlap, "only" is one crescent, "neither" is outside.
  5. Probability = number in that region ÷ total.

Notation: A ∩ B is the overlap. A ∪ B is both circles together, counted once. A' means not in A.

Why does the overlap go in first?

The 12 footballers include the 5 who also play tennis. Put 12 in the crescent and those 5 count twice. Overlap first stops that.

Another way to see it

A two-way table holds the same 20 students. Football yes or no down the side, tennis along the top. Each cell is one region: 5, 7, 4, 4.

The table stops matching with three sets. Three circles make eight regions, and a table cannot hold them.

Watch out

Writing a set's whole total in its crescent. 12 play football, but only 7 go in football only.

Leaving the outside empty. People in neither set still count. They go inside the box, outside both circles.

Try it

Answer from memory, no calculator. Give a number, or a fraction like 2/5 for a probability. The working appears only after you check.

Teach it back

25 students. 14 play chess, 10 play draughts and 4 play both. Explain to a friend who missed today how to fill in the diagram. Then how to find the probability of neither. Say it out loud, or write it down.
