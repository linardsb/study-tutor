Using equivalent ratios

By the end you can find the missing number in a ratio. You can scale a recipe or a mix up or down.

Two ratios are equivalent (worth the same) when both parts are multiplied by the same number. 3:5 and 12:20 are one comparison, written four times bigger.

3 : 5× 4 on both parts →12 : 20

Both parts multiplied by 4. That is the whole idea.

Worked example

3:5 = 12:? Find the missing number.

Step 1, line them up: 3 sits above 12, and 5 sits above the gap.
Step 2, multiplier: 12 ÷ 3 = 4.
Step 3, multiply the other part: 5 × 4 = 20.
Step 4, check: both parts went up four times, so 3:5 = 12:20.

A recipe uses 300 g of flour for 4 people. How much for 10 people? Step 2, multiplier 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5. Step 3, 300 × 2.5 = 750 g.

The method

  1. Write the ratio you know above the one you want, matching the parts.
  2. Find the multiplier: the new part divided by the part you know.
  3. Multiply the other part by the same multiplier.
  4. Check: both parts changed by the same number.

If the new ratio is smaller, the multiplier is less than 1. Dividing both parts by the same number works too.

Why is the multiplier 12 ÷ 3, and not 12 − 3?

A ratio says how many times, not how much more. 3 to 12 is four times as many, so the other part goes four times too. Adding 9 to both gives 12:14, a different comparison.

Another way to see it

Draw the two amounts as one double number line, with the parts lined up.

One step along is 3 on top and 5 below. Four steps is 12 and 20.

The line stops matching when the multiplier is not whole. Four steps land on a mark; 2.5 steps land between two.

Watch out

Multiply, do not add. 3:5 is not the same as 12:14, even though both parts went up by 9.

Match the parts. The multiplier comes from the part you know both values of.

Try it

Answer from memory. Give a number only, no units, unless the question asks for a ratio. The working appears only after you check.

1. 2:7 = ?:21. Find the missing number. The first step is done. Finish it.

Multiplier: 21 ÷ 7 = 3. …

Multiply the 2 by that multiplier.

Multiplier: 21 ÷ 7 = 3. 2 × 3 = 6.

2. 5:3 = 40:?. Find the missing number. The first step is done. Finish it.

The 5 and the 40 are the pair you know both values of. …

Work out 40 ÷ 5 first.

The 5 and the 40 are the pair you know both values of. Multiplier: 40 ÷ 5 = 8. 3 × 8 = 24.

3. A recipe for 4 people needs 300 g of flour. How many grams for 10 people?

The multiplier is 10 ÷ 4.

Multiplier: 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5. 300 × 2.5 = 750 g.

4. Simplify 18:24 to its smallest whole numbers.

Nothing is being scaled up here. What divides into both 18 and 24?

This is simplifying, not scaling. The highest common factor of 18 and 24 is 6. 18 ÷ 6 = 3 and 24 ÷ 6 = 4. Answer 3:4.

5. In a class the ratio of girls to boys is 3:4. There are 16 boys. How many girls?

Boys is the part you know both values of: 4 and 16.

Boys: 16 ÷ 4 = 4, so the multiplier is 4. Girls: 3 × 4 = 12.
