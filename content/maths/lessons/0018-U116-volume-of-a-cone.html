Volume of a cone

By the end you can use the cone formula the exam gives you, and pick the right height.

A cone is a circle base rising to a point. The exam gives you this formula: V = 1/3 πr²h. r is the radius of the base. h is the perpendicular height (straight up from the centre of the base to the point). The sloping edge is the slant height.

Radius 3 cm, perpendicular height 10 cm. r² = 9. 9 × 10 = 90. 90 ÷ 3 = 30. Volume = 30π cm³.

Where the ÷ 3 comes from

Drag the shape to turn it. Pour the cone into the tin round it: it takes three cones to fill it, so one cone is a third of the tin. Change r and h and the numbers follow.

The dashed blue line is the perpendicular height, straight up the middle. The sloping edge is never in the formula.

Worked example

A cone has radius 3 cm and perpendicular height 10 cm. Find its volume in terms of π.

Step 1, the radius: r = 3.
Step 2, square it: 3 × 3 = 9.
Step 3, times the height: 9 × 10 = 90.
Step 4, divide by 3: 90 ÷ 3 = 30.
Step 5, times π: 30π.
Step 6, the unit: 30π cm³.

The method

  1. Find the radius of the base. Halve a diameter.
  2. Square the radius: r × r.
  3. Multiply by the perpendicular height h.
  4. Divide by 3.
  5. Multiply by π. Leave π in the answer unless told to use a number.
  6. Add the unit cubed, like cm³.

Paper 1: keep π as a symbol, or use 3 if told. Calculator: type it in and round as asked. 30π = 94.2 cm³ to 1 decimal place.

Why is the slant height never in the formula?

Volume is the base area times how tall the cone stands, then a third of that. How tall it stands is the perpendicular height. The slant is the sloping edge, and it is longer.

Watch out

Do not skip the ÷ 3. πr²h is a cylinder, three times too big.

Use the perpendicular height, not the slant. The exam often prints both.

Halve a diameter before you square it.

Try it

Answer from memory, no calculator. Write pi as the word pi, like 30pi. A number is enough, or add cm3. The working appears only after you check.
