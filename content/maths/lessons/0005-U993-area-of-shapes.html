Area of shapes

By the end you can find the area of a compound shape, a triangle and a trapezium.

Area is the space inside a shape, counted in squares. A compound shape (one built from simpler shapes) splits into pieces you know.

Cut along the dashed line. The 5 cm is not printed. It is 8 − 3.

Worked example

The L-shape above.

Step 1, cut it: rectangle A on top, rectangle B along the bottom.
Step 2, missing length: 8 − 3 = 5 cm, the height of A.
Step 3, each piece: A is 4 × 5 = 20 cm², B is 10 × 3 = 30 cm².
Step 4, add: 20 + 30 = 50 cm².

A trapezium has parallel sides 6 cm and 10 cm, and height 4 cm. Step 3, ½ × (6 + 10) × 4 = 8 × 4 = 32 cm².

A triangle has base 8 cm and height 5 cm. Step 3, ½ × 8 × 5 = 4 × 5 = 20 cm².

The method

  1. Split the shape into rectangles and triangles. Draw the cut.
  2. Work out any missing length by adding or subtracting the ones given.
  3. Find the area of each piece.
  4. Add the pieces. Write the unit squared, like cm².

Rectangle: length × width. Triangle: ½ × base × height. Trapezium (one pair of parallel sides, a and b): ½ × (a + b) × h. Parallelogram: base × height.

Take-away method, also fine: the big rectangle minus the missing piece.

Why is the height of a triangle the straight-up distance, and not the sloping side?

A triangle is half a rectangle on the same base. That rectangle is as tall as the triangle stands, at a right angle to it. The sloping side is longer, so it makes the area too big.

Another way to see it

Draw the rectangle round the triangle. Its diagonal cuts it in two matching halves.

The shaded triangle is half the dashed rectangle: ½ × 8 × 5 = 20.

The halving stops matching for a trapezium. It has two different parallel sides, so average them.

Watch out

Find the missing lengths before you multiply. The exam leaves a side blank on purpose.

Height means straight up from the base, at a right angle, never the sloping side.

Try it

Answer from memory, no calculator. A number is enough, or add cm2. The working appears only after you check.

1. Find the area of this triangle. The first steps are done. Finish it.

½ × 10 × 6. Half of 10 is 5. …

Multiply your 5 by the height.

½ × 10 × 6. Half of 10 is 5. 5 × 6 = 30 cm².

2. A 12 cm by 8 cm rectangle has a 4 cm by 3 cm corner cut out. Find the area left. The first step is done. Finish it.

Big rectangle: 12 × 8 = 96 cm². …

Work out the area of the corner piece next.

Big rectangle: 12 × 8 = 96 cm². Corner piece: 4 × 3 = 12 cm². Left: 96 − 12 = 84 cm².

3. Find the area of this trapezium.

Add the two parallel sides first: 5 + 9.

½ × (5 + 9) × 6 = ½ × 14 × 6 = 7 × 6 = 42 cm².

4. Find the area of this circle. Give your answer in terms of pi, like 9pi.

This is not a compound shape. The area of a circle is pi × r × r.

A circle needs its own formula: area = pi × r². Here r = 5, so 5² = 25 and the area is 25pi cm².

5. A lawn is 8 m by 5 m, with a 2 m by 2 m patio in one corner. Turf costs £3 for each square metre. How much does turfing the rest cost, in pounds?

Find the turfed area first, in square metres.

Whole lawn: 8 × 5 = 40 m². Patio: 2 × 2 = 4 m². Turfed: 40 − 4 = 36 m². Cost: 36 × 3 = £108.
