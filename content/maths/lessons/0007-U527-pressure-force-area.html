Pressure, force and area

By the end you can find pressure, force or area from the other two, with the right unit.

Pressure is how hard a force pushes on each bit of area. Force is measured in newtons (N). The triangle holds all three versions of pressure = force ÷ area.

Cover the letter you want. F over P and A means F = P × A. P beside A means P = F ÷ A, and A = F ÷ P.

Worked example

A box pushes down with a force of 600 N on a base of area 2 m². Find the pressure.
Step 1, draw the triangle: F on top, P and A below.
Step 2, cover P.
Step 3, F is left sitting over A, so divide.
Step 4, 600 ÷ 2 = 300.
Step 5, the area was in m², so the answer is 300 N/m².

The pressure on a floor is 50 N/m² and the area is 4 m². Find the force.
Step 2, cover F.
Step 3, P and A are left side by side, so multiply.
Step 4, 50 × 4 = 200 N.

The method

  1. Draw the triangle: F on top, P and A underneath.
  2. Cover the letter the question asks for.
  3. Read what is left: side by side means multiply, one above the other means divide.
  4. Put in the numbers and work it out.
  5. Write the unit. Pressure is N/m² if the area is in m², N/cm² if it is in cm².

Without the triangle, learn the one line: pressure = force ÷ area. The other two come from rearranging it.

Why does a bigger area give a smaller pressure, when the force has not changed?

The same push is shared over more surface, so each bit gets less. That is why snowshoes stop you sinking and a drawing pin goes in.

Another way to see it

Read N/m² as a rate, like miles per hour. It is the newtons landing on each square metre.

600 N spread over 2 square metres is 300 N on each one.

The rate picture stops matching when you work backwards. To find an area from a force and a pressure, you still need the triangle.

Watch out

Force goes on top of the triangle. Swapping it with the area turns every answer upside down.

Match the units. An area in cm² gives a pressure in N/cm², not N/m².

Try it

Answer from memory, no calculator. A number is enough, or add the unit like 200 N/m2. The working appears only after you check.

Teach it back

Explain to a friend who missed today how to find the area. The force is 120 N and the pressure is 8 N/m². Say how the triangle picks the sum.
