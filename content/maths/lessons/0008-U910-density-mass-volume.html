Density, mass and volume

By the end you can find density, mass or volume from the other two, with the unit.

Mass is the amount of stuff, in grams. Volume is the space it takes up, in cm³. Density is the grams in each cm³. The triangle holds all three versions of density = mass ÷ volume.

Cover the letter you want. M over D and V means M = D × V. D beside V means D = M ÷ V, and V = M ÷ D.

Worked example

A block of metal has mass 540 g and volume 200 cm³. Find its density.
Step 1, draw the triangle: M on top, D and V below.
Step 2, the volume is given, so nothing to find first.
Step 3, cover D.
Step 4, M is left sitting over V, so divide.
Step 5, 540 ÷ 200 = 2.7.
Step 6, grams and cm³, so the answer is 2.7 g/cm³.

A metal has density 8 g/cm³. Find the mass of 50 cm³ of it.
Step 3, cover M.
Step 4, D and V are left side by side, so multiply.
Step 5, 8 × 50 = 400.
Step 6, mass is in grams: 400 g.

The method

  1. Draw the triangle: M on top, D and V underneath.
  2. If only the sides of a cuboid are given, find the volume first: length × width × height.
  3. Cover the letter the question asks for.
  4. Read what is left: side by side means multiply, one above the other means divide.
  5. Put in the numbers and work it out.
  6. Write the unit. Grams with cm³ gives g/cm³. Kilograms with m³ gives kg/m³.
Why does the unit g/cm³ tell you which way round to divide?

Read it aloud: grams per cubic centimetre. Per means divided by. So density is mass divided by volume.

Another way to see it

Cut the block into 1 cm³ cubes. Density is the grams in each single cube. 540 g shared across 200 cubes is 2.7 g each.

Each cube holds 2.7 g. 200 of them hold 540 g.

The cubes stop matching when you have density and mass and want the volume. Then you need the triangle: 540 ÷ 2.7.

Watch out

Mass goes on top. Swapping it with the volume turns every answer upside down.

A cuboid question hides a step. Multiply the three sides to get the volume before you touch the triangle.

Try it

Answer from memory, no calculator. A number is enough, or add the unit like 2 g/cm3. The working appears only after you check.

Teach it back

A block is 2 cm by 3 cm by 5 cm and weighs 240 g. Explain to a friend who missed today how to find its density. Say what comes before the triangle. Say it out loud, or write it down.
