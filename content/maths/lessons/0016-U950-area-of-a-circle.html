Area of a circle

By the end you can find a circle's area from its radius or diameter, leaving π in.

The radius r runs from the centre to the edge. The diameter d runs right across, through the centre, and is twice the radius. The formula uses r, so halve a diameter first.

Radius 5 cm, diameter 10 cm. Area = π × 5 × 5 = 25π cm².

Worked example

A circle has diameter 8 cm. Find its area in terms of π.

Step 1, the radius: 8 ÷ 2 = 4 cm.
Step 2, square it: 4 × 4 = 16.
Step 3, times π: 16 × π = 16π.
Step 4, the unit: 16π cm².

The same circle, taking π as 3. Step 3 becomes 16 × 3 = 48 cm².

The method

  1. Find the radius. If the question gives the diameter, halve it.
  2. Square the radius: r × r.
  3. Multiply by π. Write the answer as a number times π, like 25π.
  4. Add the unit squared, like cm².

A = πr². Paper 1: leave π in, or use 3 if told. Calculator: press the π key and round as asked: 25π = 78.5 cm².

Why square the radius, and not double it?

Area counts squares. A square of side r fits in one corner, and the circle covers π of them. Doubling r gives a length, not an area.

Another way to see it

Cut the circle into thin slices and lay them head to tail. That makes a near rectangle, r tall and πr wide.

Its area is πr × r. It stops matching at the wobbly top and bottom: only very thin slices make a true rectangle.

Watch out

Halve the diameter first. Using 8 as the radius gives 64π, four times too big.

Square the radius, do not double it.

πd and 2πr give the circumference (the distance round the edge), not the area.

Try it

Answer from memory, no calculator. Write pi as the word pi, like 25pi. A number is enough, or add cm2. The working appears only after you check.

Teach it back

A friend missed today. Explain how to find the area of a circle with diameter 14 cm, π left in. Say it out loud, or write it down.
