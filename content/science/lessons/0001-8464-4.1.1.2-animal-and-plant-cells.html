Animal and plant cells

By the end you can name the parts of animal and plant cells and say what each one does. You can also find them on a diagram.

A model animal cell is a simple picture of the parts most animal cells share. There are four:

  • Cytoplasm: a jelly with nutrients and salts dissolved in it. Many chemical reactions happen here.
  • Cell membrane: the thin layer around the cell. It controls what goes in and out.
  • Nucleus: holds the genetic material, which controls what the cell does.
  • Mitochondria: where respiration releases most of the cell's energy.

A plant cell has all four, plus three more:

  • Cell wall: made of cellulose. It makes the cell stronger and holds the plant up.
  • Chloroplasts: contain chlorophyll, a green pigment that absorbs light for photosynthesis.
  • Permanent vacuole: filled with cell sap. It keeps the cell firm.

Real cells are often specialised. Their shape, size and parts suit the job they do. Nerve cells and blood cells are specialised animal cells; root hair cells and palisade cells are specialised plant cells.

Naming the parts on a diagram

  1. Start at the outside. One thin layer means an animal cell. Two layers, with a thick one outside, means a plant cell: the cell wall, then the membrane.
  2. Find the biggest round body inside. That is the nucleus.
  3. Small green bodies are chloroplasts. Only plant cells have them.
  4. A large space in the middle of a plant cell is the permanent vacuole.
  5. Everything else inside the membrane is cytoplasm.

Watch out

The model cell is a simplified picture. Many real cells look different because they are specialised.

Not every plant cell has chloroplasts. A root hair cell has none.

A palisade cell is a specialised cell too, even though textbook diagrams of a plant cell often look like one.

Respiration does not create energy. It transfers energy the cell can use.
